HOW IT WORKS

  1. You hand it to your coding agent

    Claude Code, Codex, or anything that takes a prompt. It clones the repo, walks you through a two-minute Slack app, and starts the bird.

  2. The bird watches your Slack

    Direct messages and @mentions only. It waits 25 to 90 seconds, because nobody answers in 200 milliseconds, then replies as you.

  3. You play GTA VI

    If you put the controller down and type in a conversation yourself, the bird backs off. After eight hours it clocks off. It has better boundaries than you do.

TWO MODES

Y mode

The original. Every message gets the same answer. Historically accurate, professionally fatal.

dana: can you take the 4pm with legal?
you: y
dana: and own the migration?
you: y

Agent mode

Your coding agent writes one short holding reply. It never agrees to a deadline, a meeting or a decision. It knows nothing and commits to less.

dana: did you get a chance to look at the Q4 deck?
you: haven't gotten to it yet, been swamped but will take a look soon
sam: thoughts on the new onboarding flow?
you: in the middle of something, will come back to this

REACTOR SAFETY

The last time someone left a drinking bird in charge, it fell over and the core nearly melted. This bird knows its limits. For these messages it says nothing, rings a bell on your laptop and waits for a human:

  • Anything urgent, on fire, or down
  • “Quick call?” and its relatives
  • “Are you there?”
  • “Is this a bot?”
  • “Are you playing GTA?”
  • Requests to approve, merge or sign off
  • Passwords, payments and other secrets
  • Anything with HR in it
  • The fourth message in an hour from the same person

QUESTIONS

Is this a good idea?
No.
Why not just send a fake sick note?
Everyone on your team is sending one. Somebody has to look like they are at work. A sick note also runs out after a day, and the map does not.
Does it work after launch day?
Yes. It works any day. It was built for one.
Do you host it? Do you see my messages?
No. It runs on your machine, with a Slack app you create in your own workspace. There is no server. We could not read your messages if we wanted to, and we do not want to.
Does it keep my status dot green?
No. Slack decides that from real activity. The bird can answer for you. It cannot be you.
Will my colleagues notice?
Eventually. That is how the episode ends, too.
Can a colleague trick it with a clever message?
The agent runs with no tools, in an empty folder, and anything longer than one plain line is thrown away. They can at most make it say something vague, which is what it does anyway.
Will I get fired?
The licence says “without warranty of any kind”. That covers your job.
